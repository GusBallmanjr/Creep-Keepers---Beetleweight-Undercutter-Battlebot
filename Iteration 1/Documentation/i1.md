# Creep Keepers — Beetleweight Undercutter  
## Iteration 1 (Fall 2025 → Completed November 2025)
## Nickname - "Eduardo"

Iteration 1 (Eduardo) was the first competitive version following the early‑fall prototype developed by **Gus Ballman, Carson Roskelley, Jayden Claytor, and Natalie Massarenti**. This build focused on refining the chassis, improving internal layout, and preparing the robot for its first full event. While the bot successfully entered competition, several structural and weight‑related issues limited overall performance. 
---

## Version 1 Summary

+ **Competition Record:** 1–2  
+ **Weigh‑In:** 2.99 lb  
+ **Completion Date:** November 2025  
+ **Armor Status:** Wheel‑side armor removed due to weight constraints  

---

## Key Issues Identified

Iteration 1 revealed several critical design flaws that directly impacted performance and reliability:

### **1. Incorrect Material Selection in CAD**
The CAD model used the wrong aluminum alloy (has since been changed), causing the predicted mass to be significantly lower than the real manufactured part.  
> From the document: “CAD model of chassis used an incorrect aluminum alloy causing mass issues”

### **2. Weight Overruns**
The robot exceeded the 3 lb limit until armor was removed.  
> “Armor had to be removed to meet the 3 lb weight limit”

### **3. Insufficient Structural Stiffness**
The frame lacked the rigidity required for an undercutter weapon system, reducing energy transfer and increasing vibration.  
> “Structural stiffness was insufficient for an undercutter weapon system”

### **4. Inefficient Material Usage**
Despite using more material than the prototype, the structure provided less support.  
> “Overall design used more material but provided less structural support”

---

## Lessons Learned

Iteration 1 provided several important takeaways that shaped future revisions:

+ **Material selection must balance strength, machinability, and density**  
+ **Weight budgeting must begin early in the design process**  
_ **Undercutters require a rigid frame to transfer weapon energy effectively**

> These points are drawn directly from the “Lessons Learned” section of the document.
